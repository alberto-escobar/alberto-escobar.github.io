# Review of my second year in BCS

I completed my second year of BCS and wanted to provide a review of the courses I took during 2024W1 and 2024W2. For each course I give scores based on the following aspects:

- **Utility**: How useful the course was for later courses or projects
- **Interest**: How interesting the course was
- **Difficulty**: How difficult the course was

## 2024W1

### CPSC 304 - Introduction to Relational Databases
This course is an introduction to relational databases and covered database design, schema refinement, normalization, formal query language, SQL, data warehousing, and data mining. The components of this course consisted of inclass exercises, a midterm, a final exam, and a major project. 

I was taught by Rachel Potiger, who honestly was one of the best professors to have for this course. Firstly her research is in databases and she also is very open to undergraduate students participating in her research reading group sessions. Secondly she teaches the material with lots of examples and articulates the material well. Lastly she is very kind and caring of her students and will periodical pause in lecture to check if students are understanding or if they are lost, and if the latter will take the time to rexplain the concepts again.

This course started out rough for me, I almost failed the first midterm. This part was on me I chose to not attend class regularly and not review notes consistently. The struggle with this course is that I did not take the time to learn the mindset you have to adopt when doing data base design and refinement of schemas. A lot of it is abstract and that can be difficult to grasp but gets easier as you make sense of concepts and terms that are tossed around. After being humbled by the midterm I dedicated a lot of time to catch up and to do well on the material being taught and I succeeding in scoring A at the end of the term. The take away is that you cannot be absent in those course, you should regularly attend lecture or review notes and understand what is happening.

The major project is a creating an novel app with a sophisticated database design within a group of 2-3 students. a Novel app here means that you had to propose your app idea with the TAs to ensure it was novel and not something that is on or similar to an app idea on a black list they had. This limitation really got the creativity going as we had to think about some kind of problem we encountered in everyday life and build something to address it. Our group built a recipe sharing application that allowed users to create and share reciepes with friends and also schedule reciepes and cooks for home cooking. The project is not too difficult if you are knowledgable of how to connect together a database to a backend system and have that backend talk to a frontend.

I believe this course is very interesting as it makes you rexamine how you would efficently and properly store data in a database. I personally have reviewed past projects I built and realized what a mess my database is and how I would redesign it. The project can be fun and also a good way to throw something on to you resume. The usefulness of this course I think is high as software systems and data systems utlize relation databases highly so having knowledge of how to design and build a relation database for a given situation is very valuable.

- **Utility**: 5/5
- **Interest**: 4/5
- **Difficulty**: 3/5

### CPSC 313 - Computer Hardware and Operating Systems
This course is like CPSC 213 but you actually learn useful concepts relating to how you go from hardware to operating systems. This course covers how CPUs implement and execute instructions, caches and memory systems, disks and file systems, virtual memory, operating systems, and virtual machines. The components of the courses are various class exercises (stuff you do before, during, and after lecture), weekly take home labs, biweekly quizes, and a final exam.

This course was taught by not 1 but 3 professors. Steve Wolfman, Patrice Belleville, and Reto Achermann. All three are extremely knowledgable of operating systems and computer architecture and also very friendly and approchable if you have questions about the material.

This course was dense. Extremely dense. But very interesting and I learned a great deal about computer systems through the constant grind of studying regularly. I also learned quite a bit about x86 through their introduction of the educational ISA that is cunningly named y86. While this course was very hard, the lectures along with the class exercises really were just reptitions of the topic being taught in various mediums to help absorb it. I think the professors did an excellent job using Margo Seltzers videos for introducing the material as pre class exercises. Here is a link to the videos if you are interested: https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL5U42w7ah5Jx9iD73Cgd8dXc4mF8a2Uek

An interesting exercise I enjoyed in this course was how they would show C code and assembly code side by side to show how the italics: very readable c code is converest to less readable assembly, but if you work through the assmebly you can recreate the c code (after several headaches of course!).  

Like I mentioned before, if you have an interest in systems, this material will be interesting and relevent in higher level courses and in your career. However if you are more of a data person or design person then this course is really mor eof a hurdle instead of foundational.

- **Utility**: 4/5
- **Interest**: 5/5
- **Difficulty**: 5/5

### CPSC 317 - Introduction to Computer Networking
This course is a through introduction to computer networking, the course essentially walked me through each level of the OSI stack indepth. The course components consisted of 5 assignments (of varying difficulty), 4 Quizzes, class pariticpation through iclicker, and a final exam.

The course was taught by Maryam Aliabadi. She did a good job in delivering the material, she would take the time to carefully walk through examples. This was very needed as I was in the 9AM section and was very sleepy during some lectures. It helps that this course is very well established at UBC and she is ver involved with the cloud innovation center.

The course overall was fairly standard. I found the material interesting but a bit dry at times. At some point in semester, the material clicked together and I really understood how the OSI model works. The trick was to regularly go back to previous matrerial in the course and understand how it connects to topics being taught now. The assignments for the most part are straight forward, however one assignment was very brutal. This particular assigned required us to implement TCP in C. This was very difficult and required a lot of collaboration. Personally I think that if you are systems oriented person, learning about networks is essential for day to day. 

I can confidently say that my understanding of troubleshooting network problems I was having with my projects after taking the course made it easier to comprehend what was happening, and this material would be useful to know when taking later systems or cybersecturity courses.


- **Utility**: 4/5
- **Interest**: 4/5
- **Difficulty**: 3/5


### CPSC 340 - Machine Learning and Data Mining
This course is a very good fundementals course on machine learning. This course begins how to do data exploration, then dives into various classifer models, clustering models, regression, PCA, feature engineering, and ends on neural networks and touches on CNN, RNNs. The course struture was very standard and consited of 6 assignments, a midterm, a final, and an optional research project (I recommend trying it because it is a good way to expand your knowledge on the topic).

For this semester I had the immense pleasure to be taught by Jef Clune, and Cong Lu. Both of them are predominent researchers in AI, and excellent lectures. They would take the time to explain concepts, check in with the class on understand and continue exampling if there was misunderstandings. Attending office hours was also great as they have a wealth of information to share about the field.

This course surprisingly is the least applicable to me as I am more of a systems person instead of a data science person, but it was very informative and provided me a good expansion on my scope of understanding of machine learning. With the predominance of AI for everyday use and using ML moels as a way to make data driven decisions in code, learning this stuff was very useful and helped me catch up to what is a rapidly accelerating field. It also was a dense course, The was tested on my knowledge in linear algebra, probability, and statisitcs and it covered a lot of topics, and each topic while indepth only scratched the surface of an iceberg of information I could explore. It was a hard course, and I spent a lot of time studying and working on assignments, but it is one of my favorite I have taken at UBC. I also opted to do the optional research assignment, which I had no regrets doing. The project was writing a literature review paper on an topic of machine learning. While the course much direct utility to my interests, I think it can be useful for better understanding the world of ML which is generally shrowded in mystery. I think taking the course the Jeff Clune and Cong Lu was what really made me consider this one of my favorite courses at UBC.

- **Utility**: 4/5
- **Interest**: 6/5
- **Difficulty**: 4/5
